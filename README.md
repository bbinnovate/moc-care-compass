# Moc Care Compass

Create a new polished, modern, premium homepage concept for MOC India (M | O | C Cancer Care & Research Centre) to present as a design pitch to the client. This is a visual/UI prototype, not a production medical booking system. The primary outcome is to establish trust, help patients and caregivers quickly understand MOC’s cancer-care services, discover doctors and centres, understand the care journey, and find a clear next step.

Use the existing MOC India website https://www.mocindia.co.in/ as the source of truth for logo, brand colours, existing services, and wording. Inspect the existing website and preserve the current brand colour direction instead of inventing a different identity. Create a tasteful, contemporary system around those existing colours: clean warm-white backgrounds, deep brand colour for key text and actions, restrained accent usage, generous whitespace, refined typography, clear hierarchy, subtle borders and shadows, elegant but approachable cards, and high-quality healthcare photography. It should feel confident, caring, human, credible and premium—not like a generic hospital template, corporate dashboard, overly sterile clinic, luxury spa, or tech startup. Do not use a dark full-page theme. Use natural, documentary-feeling photos of doctors talking with patients, care teams, and welcoming treatment spaces. Avoid sad/crying cancer imagery, overly staged handshakes, stock-photo clichés, scary medical imagery, and images that imply guaranteed outcomes. Use real MOC logo/assets from its official site when feasible; do not create a new logo.

Use these reference sites for patterns only, never copy their layouts verbatim:
- HCG Oncology https://www.hcgoncology.com/ — specialist cancer-care positioning, find doctors/specialities/hospitals, confidence and service discovery.
- Cancer Mitr https://www.cancermitr.com/ — patient-oriented content, cancer journey and accessible resources.
- American Cancer Society https://www.cancer.org/ — patient education and support-resource organization.
- MD Anderson https://www.mdanderson.org/ — clear treatment information and patient/family journey.
- Apollo Hospitals https://www.apollohospitals.com/ — familiar doctor discovery and appointment navigation.
Supporting references: Indian Cancer Society https://www.indiancancersociety.org, HCG Hospitals https://www.hcghospitals.com/, CancerCare https://www.cancercare.org/, Medanta https://www.medanta.org/, Lilavati Hospital https://www.lilavatihospital.com/, Hinduja Hospital https://www.hindujahospital.com/, Narayana Health https://www.narayanahealth.org/, and Nanavati Max Hospital https://www.nanavatimaxhospital.org/. Current MOC pages: https://www.mocindia.co.in/, https://www.mocindia.co.in/about-moc-india, https://www.mocindia.co.in/all/centers/details.

Build the homepage in this exact order:
1. Header & Navigation
2. Hero Section
3. Why MOC
4. Treatments & Services
5. Your Cancer Care Journey
6. Meet Our Specialists
7. Find an MOC Centre
8. Patient Stories & Experiences
9. Patient Education & Resources
10. Appointment CTA
11. Footer

Header:
- Provide a slim utility row only if it improves usability; include phone/contact access if verified on the current site.
- Main navigation: Treatments, Find a Doctor, Our Centres, Patient Resources, About MOC.
- One clearly dominant action at right: “Request an Appointment” or “Book Appointment” only if the current real workflow supports direct booking. Since booking workflow is not confirmed, use “Request an Appointment” as the pitch CTA and make it open a clear enquiry/callback modal or form prototype, with no claims that a booking is confirmed.
- Ensure mobile menu is considered and navigation is accessible.

Hero:
- Create a visually striking split layout or carefully composed wide image layout that immediately communicates specialist cancer care with empathy and confidence.
- Suggested direction for headline: “Cancer care, with clarity and compassion.” Do not overpromise clinical results.
- Supporting copy should be concise and plain-spoken: MOC helps patients access specialist cancer care, treatment guidance and support across its centres. Edit against the actual source wording.
- Two actions maximum: primary “Request an Appointment”, secondary text/outline “Find a Centre”. Do NOT add three or four competing CTAs or another “How can we help you today?” action grid immediately below; avoid repetitive action blocks.
- It can include a discreet search or service shortcut only if it is genuinely useful and does not duplicate the CTAs.
- Visual must feel designed, not like a template: balanced type scale, asymmetrical but calm composition, refined cropping, strong spacing, brand-colour accent detail.

Why MOC:
- A compact trust-building section with 3 or 4 benefit points supported by the current site, such as specialist oncology expertise, nearby centres, coordinated care, and patient support. Use careful language and avoid generic “best/world-class” claims unless directly supported.
- MOC’s current site contains inconsistent statistics/claims on different sections (e.g., patient totals and centre counts). Do NOT reproduce statistics, numerical claims, accreditation claims, or outcome rates unless checked for consistency and clearly supported. Prefer no large number-stat band in the pitch.
- The client has indicated not to make “affordable cancer care” a headline positioning point; do not make affordability the visual or copy focus even if older site copy mentions it.

Treatments & Services:
- Present the actual, source-backed MOC services in a clean grid of about 6 items, with editorial-style icons or tasteful imagery and short descriptions. The official site mentions services such as Chemotherapy, Immunotherapy, Targeted Therapy, Clinical Research, Palliative Care, Genetic Counselling, Psycho-Oncology, Onco Nutrition, Onco Physiotherapy, Patient Support Group, and other services. Do not imply MOC provides surgical or radiation treatment directly unless it is clearly framed as coordinated/partner care and supported by the current site. A “View all treatments and services” link can lead to a placeholder listing page.
- Avoid a cluttered alphabet soup of treatment names. Use consistent cards and simple wording.

Your Cancer Care Journey:
- Use a clear, reassuring, horizontal stepper on desktop and a stacked/vertical flow on mobile. It explains what a patient may experience: Speak to the team / Request an appointment → Consult a specialist → Evaluation and treatment planning → Treatment and supportive care → Follow-up and ongoing guidance. Phrase it as a general guide that can vary by patient and care plan, not as a guaranteed pathway. The current site already features “Your Journey at MOC”; improve clarity rather than implying this content is entirely new. No “Become cancer-free” promise.

Meet Our Specialists:
- Show a small set of doctor cards with image, verified name, speciality, and centre/locations only if visible on the official site and can be verified. If reliable real data cannot be retrieved, use clearly labelled “Preview profile” placeholders, not made-up doctors or biographies. Cards should feel premium and informative, not like e-commerce cards.
- Include a “Meet all specialists” link.

Find an MOC Centre:
- Make this section prominent because the client notes emphasised location/accessibility and being able to find a nearby centre.
- Use a functional front-end search field (city/area or PIN code) with a small set of source-backed sample centre cards/locations from the official website, such as relevant Mumbai/Pune/Gujarat locations only if current site data supports them. Add directions/contact action only when links/details exist. If a complete live locator is not feasible, make a convincing interactive prototype with filters and clear sample/demo data labels rather than pretending it is connected to a real database.
- Do not make an inaccurate map or fake travel distances.

Patient Stories & Experiences:
- Design one large editorial feature and 2 smaller cards or a well-designed media carousel. Only use genuine published MOC stories/videos if accessible. Otherwise show clearly labelled demo placeholders and do not invent quotes, names, diagnoses, outcomes or testimonials. Respect patient consent and dignity.

Patient Education & Resources:
- Include patient education articles, doctor videos, awareness resources and FAQs in a calm, readable layout. Use existing MOC awareness/blog topics where available; titles may be drawn from current site only. Never present the design demo as clinical advice. Include “Reviewed by” only when genuine reviewer information is available.

Appointment CTA:
- Strong final invitation with one action: “Request an Appointment” and a supporting phone/contact option if verified.
- The enquiry form/modal must be a functional UI prototype with name, contact number, preferred location, and optional message; validation and success state should work in the frontend, but clearly state “Demo only — your request has not been sent” and do not collect/store data or connect to a backend.

Footer:
- Logo, concise positioning sentence, main navigation, treatment/services links, patient resources, contact details and policy links sourced from official website. Avoid overwhelming multi-column link dumps. Include appropriate copyright.

Interaction and quality requirements:
- Responsive desktop/tablet/mobile design. Check full-page visual consistency.
- Implement working front-end interactions for mobile menu, mega menu/dropdowns as appropriate, location search/filter, doctor/service card links, resource tabs if used, and appointment enquiry modal with validation/demo-only state.
- Use subtle motion only where it supports understanding; honor reduced-motion preferences.
- Accessibility: semantic headings, keyboard focus styles, sufficient contrast, meaningful alt text, labelled form fields, clear tap targets and no colour-only communication.
- Avoid giant rounded cards everywhere, excessive pill buttons, floating widgets, gradient-heavy backgrounds, unnecessary counters, fake certifications, fake clinical stats, fake testimonials, unverified doctor credentials, or generic paragraphs.
- Visual quality should be close to a considered healthcare brand redesign by a good agency. The result is a clickable, client-presentation-ready homepage prototype, not a final medically approved website.
- Keep copy concise and natural, not AI-sounding. When factual data is uncertain, make it an editable placeholder and document it visibly or in code comments; do not silently invent facts.
Use the provided homepage flow and make informed design decisions without asking for more input.
